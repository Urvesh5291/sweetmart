# Patel Sweet Mart --- Digital Brand Guide

## Website Design System & Brand Standards

**Brand:** પટેલ સ્વીટ માર્ટ / Patel Sweet Mart\
**Established:** 1995\
**Guide Version:** 1.0\
**Purpose:** World-class website, e-commerce, campaign, and digital
product design system.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1. Brand Vision

Patel Sweet Mart should feel like a **modern Gujarati food
institution**: rooted in Indian culture, trusted across generations,
energetic enough for younger customers, and polished enough to scale
nationally and internationally.

The digital identity must balance five ideas:

**Heritage × Trust × Celebration × Youth × Premium Craft**

The website must **not** look like a generic local mithai shop. It
should feel like an established food brand being designed for its next
generation.

### Brand Promise

> **પરંપરાનો સ્વાદ. નવી પેઢી માટે.**\
> *Tradition, made for a new generation.*

### Brand Personality

-   Proudly Gujarati
-   Warm and celebratory
-   Contemporary
-   Premium, never flashy
-   Trustworthy
-   Generous
-   Food-first
-   Family-friendly
-   Youth-aware
-   Globally presentable

------------------------------------------------------------------------

**Patel Sweet Mart**

# 3. Logo System

The identity is built around a **minimal lotus-inspired Gujarati
emblem** paired with the Gujarati wordmark.

The symbol should communicate: - Indian heritage - Celebration - Craft -
Growth - Purity - Premium gifting

## Primary Logo

Preferred configuration:

**Lotus Symbol + પટેલ સ્વીટ માર્ટ**

Use this version for: - Website header - Storefront - Premium
packaging - Main advertising - Brand presentations

## Secondary Logo

**Lotus Symbol + પટેલ સ્વીટ માર્ટ + ESTD. 1995**

Use for: - About page - Heritage communication - Premium boxes -
Corporate materials

## Icon Mark

Use the lotus symbol alone for: - Favicon - App icon - Social avatar -
Browser icon - Packaging seals - Small digital badges

## Logo Clear Space

Maintain clear space around the logo equal to approximately the height
of one central lotus petal.

No typography, imagery, borders, buttons, or patterns should enter this
area.

## Minimum Digital Size

-   Full logo: approximately **140 px minimum width**
-   Symbol: approximately **24 px minimum**
-   Favicon: create dedicated simplified 16 px / 32 px versions

## Logo Rules

Never: - Stretch or compress the logo - Add uncontrolled gradients - Add
drop shadows to the production logo - Rotate the symbol - Change
individual petal colors randomly - Place it on visually noisy
photography without protection - Add extra Gujarati ornamentation around
it - Outline the entire wordmark - Use metallic effects as the default
web logo

The production logo should ultimately be created as clean vector artwork
(SVG), not a raster rendering.

# 4. Master Color Palette

## Royal Cobalt --- Primary

**HEX:** `#173F8A`\
**RGB:** `23, 63, 138`

### Meaning

Trust, strength, modernity, recognition and premium confidence.

### Use

-   Main brand color
-   Navigation
-   Primary CTA
-   Headings
-   Footer
-   Logo
-   Product packaging
-   Links and selected states

Royal Cobalt should be the most recognizable digital brand color.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Kesari Orange --- Secondary

**HEX:** `#F47B20`\
**RGB:** `244, 123, 32`

### Meaning

Energy, celebration, warmth, Indian culture and appetite.

### Use

-   Secondary CTA
-   New-product indicators
-   Promotional highlights
-   Hover accents
-   Festival campaigns
-   Small illustration details

Do not flood entire pages with orange.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Rose --- Festive Accent

**HEX:** `#CE496B`\
**RGB:** `206, 73, 107`

### Meaning

Celebration, gifting, youthfulness and warmth.

### Use

-   Gifting
-   Wedding collections
-   Festival campaigns
-   Decorative accents
-   Select badges
-   Seasonal storytelling

Rose is an accent, not a replacement for Cobalt.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Almond --- Primary Canvas

**HEX:** `#F5E6CB`\
**RGB:** `245, 230, 203`

### Meaning

Warmth, softness, hospitality and premium food culture.

### Use

-   Page backgrounds
-   Product storytelling
-   Cards
-   Editorial sections
-   Packaging backgrounds
-   Negative space

Prefer Almond over sterile pure white when appropriate.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Charcoal --- Neutral

**HEX:** `#242424`\
**RGB:** `36, 36, 36`

### Meaning

Clarity, sophistication, contrast and contemporary structure.

### Use

-   Body copy
-   Dark sections
-   Footer alternatives
-   Icons
-   Supporting UI

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Heritage Gold --- Luxury Accent

**HEX:** `#D4AF37`\
**RGB:** `212, 175, 55`

### Meaning

Heritage, celebration, craft and premium gifting.

### Use

-   Fine borders
-   Premium icons
-   Dividers
-   Selected packaging details
-   Heritage storytelling

Gold should be used sparingly. On physical packaging, define a real
metallic foil/Pantone specification separately. `#D4AF37` is only the
digital approximation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 5. Recommended Color Proportion

A useful starting ratio for the complete identity:

  Color             Approx. Share
  --------------- ---------------
  Royal Cobalt                30%
  Almond                      25%
  Kesari Orange               20%
  Rose                        15%
  Charcoal                     8%
  Heritage Gold                2%

This is a brand-system ratio, **not a requirement that every webpage
contain all six colors**.

For premium pages, increase Almond/neutral space and reduce accent
colors.

# 7. Typography

Typography must communicate two worlds:

**Gujarati cultural confidence + contemporary digital clarity.**

## Gujarati

Use Gujarati prominently in: - Hero messaging - Brand storytelling -
Festival communication - Cultural campaigns - Selected section titles

Choose a professional Gujarati typeface with excellent readability. Do
not use an ornamental Gujarati font for long paragraphs or UI labels.

## English

Use two complementary families:

### Display

A refined serif or culturally sympathetic display family for: -
Editorial headlines - Heritage storytelling - Campaign statements

### UI / Sans Serif

A highly readable modern sans-serif for: - Navigation - Buttons -
Product information - Prices - Filters - Forms - Checkout - Account
pages

## Suggested Scale

``` text
Display XL    64–80px desktop / 42–52px mobile
H1            52–64px desktop / 36–44px mobile
H2            40–48px desktop / 30–36px mobile
H3            28–34px
H4            22–26px
Body Large    18–20px
Body          16–18px
Small         14px
Caption       12–13px
```

Use responsive `clamp()` values in production instead of rigid
breakpoints wherever practical.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 8. Layout Philosophy

The website should feel **editorial, spacious and food-led**.

Use: - Large whitespace - Strong grids - Generous photography - Clear
hierarchy - Deliberate asymmetry - Rounded geometry in moderation -
Large product moments - Short, confident copy

Avoid: - Crowded category grids - Excessive banners - Constant sale
graphics - Too many borders - Multiple competing CTAs - Tiny product
photography - Overdecorated Indian motifs

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 9. Grid System

## Desktop

-   Maximum content width: approximately `1440px`
-   Standard content width: `1200–1320px`
-   12-column grid
-   24--32px gutters
-   64--96px outer margins where viewport allows

## Tablet

-   8-column grid
-   24px margins

## Mobile

-   4-column grid
-   16--20px side margins

## Section Spacing

Desktop: `96–160px`

Mobile: `64–96px`

Premium design requires breathing room.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 10. Shape Language

## Cards

Recommended: `16–24px` radius

## Buttons

Two possible systems: - Soft premium pill: `999px` - Modern premium
rounded rectangle: `12–16px`

Choose one primary system and remain consistent.

## Imagery

Image masks can occasionally reference: - Gujarati arches - Lotus
petals - Stepwell geometry

These shapes should be simplified and contemporary.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 11. Gujarati Visual Motifs

Use Indian/Gujarati culture through **abstraction**, not decoration
overload.

Potential inspiration: - Lotus geometry - Stepwell architecture - Jaali
patterns - Temple/heritage arches - Bandhani dot rhythms - Toran
geometry - Traditional textile repetition - Subtle rangoli symmetry

Patterns should generally remain around **3--8% visual prominence** in
normal UI.

Festival campaigns may intentionally increase this.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 12. Photography Direction

Food is the hero.

## Photography Characteristics

-   Rich macro detail
-   Natural texture
-   Controlled warm lighting
-   Premium plating
-   Real ingredients
-   Clean styling
-   High contrast without artificial oversaturation
-   Human warmth when lifestyle photography is used

## Priority Subjects

-   Kaju katli
-   Motichoor ladoo
-   Peda
-   Dry-fruit sweets
-   Gujarati farsan
-   Namkeen
-   Gift boxes
-   Ingredient closeups
-   Hands serving/sharing food
-   Festival tables
-   Store craftsmanship

## Avoid

-   Generic stock imagery
-   Artificially glowing food
-   Excessive props
-   Extremely yellow lighting
-   Visually messy counters
-   Low-resolution product photography
-   Different photographic styles on every page

# 13. Iconography

Icons should be: - Minimal - Rounded or gently geometric - Consistent
stroke width - Easy to recognize at small sizes

Potential branded categories: - Mithai - Namkeen - Gifting - Dry
fruits - Bakery - Delivery - Vegetarian - Premium ingredients - Stores -
Celebration

Use Cobalt/Charcoal by default. Gold is reserved for premium or heritage
contexts.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 14. Motion System

The brand should move with confidence, not noise.

Recommended: - 200--350ms UI transitions - Soft easing - Small card
lift - Image scale around 1.02--1.04 on hover - Gentle content reveals -
Controlled parallax on editorial imagery - Subtle line/motif drawing -
Refined menu transitions

Avoid: - Bouncing - Constant animation - Excessive particles -
Flashing - Aggressive zooming - Fake gold shimmer everywhere

Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.

# 20. E-Commerce UX Principles

The premium brand experience must continue through checkout.

Priorities: - Fast pages - Obvious pricing - Transparent delivery
information - Clear weight selection - Easy cart editing - Guest
checkout - Strong mobile experience - Reliable search - Useful filters -
Clear payment states - Strong empty/error states - Accessible forms -
Persistent cart - Trust signals without visual clutter

Do not sacrifice conversion clarity for visual experimentation.

# 22. Packaging-to-Web Consistency

Physical and digital experiences must share recognizable DNA.

A customer who sees a Patel Sweet Mart gift box should immediately
recognize the website.

Maintain: - Cobalt identity - Almond canvas - Kesari/Rose accents -
Lotus geometry - Consistent typography - Similar photography -
Consistent tone of voice

Do not create an unrelated visual identity for the website.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 23. Tone of Voice

## Voice

**Warm. Proud. Clear. Generous. Modern Gujarati.**

The brand should sound confident without sounding corporate.

## Example

Instead of:

> We manufacture high-quality traditional Indian sweets.

Prefer:

> Gujarati favourites, crafted with care and made for sharing.

Instead of:

> Buy our products now.

Prefer:

> Bring home something worth celebrating.

Avoid exaggerated claims such as "world's best" unless independently
supportable.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 24. Bilingual Content Strategy

Gujarati should add cultural meaning rather than simply duplicate every
English sentence.

Recommended: - Gujarati hero lines - Gujarati campaign language -
English commerce/UI where it improves clarity - Optional full Gujarati
language experience - Accurate professional translation - Consistent
product naming

Do not rely on machine-generated Gujarati without editorial review.

# 26. Accessibility

Premium means usable by more people.

Target **WCAG 2.2 AA** as the implementation baseline.

Requirements: - Keyboard navigation - Visible focus states - Semantic
headings - Alt text - Proper labels - Sufficient contrast - Minimum
usable touch targets - Reduced-motion support - Error messages that do
not depend only on color - Accessible cart and checkout - Gujarati text
tested across devices

Never assume a palette combination is accessible purely because it looks
readable.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 27. Responsive Design

Design **mobile-first**, then expand.

Important breakpoints can be implementation-specific, but common design
checks include:

``` text
Mobile       320–767
Tablet       768–1023
Desktop      1024–1439
Large        1440+
```

Test real content rather than designing only for fixed artboards.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 28. Website Performance

The premium feeling disappears if the website is slow.

Targets should include: - Modern image formats - Responsive images -
Lazy loading below the fold - Limited font weights - Font subsetting
where possible - Optimized SVG - Minimal unnecessary JavaScript -
CDN/caching - Stable layout - Fast product pages

Monitor Core Web Vitals during development.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 29. SEO & Structured Content

Every major product/category should have: - Unique title - Meta
description - Clean URL - Product schema where applicable -
Breadcrumbs - Descriptive alt text - Useful category copy - Internal
linking - Gujarati/English localization strategy - Canonical/hreflang
strategy when multiple language URLs are implemented

Do not bury important product information inside images.

# 31. Do / Don't

## DO

-   Make food photography the hero
-   Give Gujarati typography room
-   Use Cobalt consistently
-   Preserve generous Almond negative space
-   Use Kesari strategically
-   Reserve Rose for warmth/festivity
-   Keep Gold rare
-   Build reusable UI components
-   Design mobile-first
-   Make checkout extremely clear
-   Keep cultural references sophisticated
-   Tell the 1995 story meaningfully

## DON'T

-   Turn every section blue
-   Use six colors simultaneously everywhere
-   Make Gold a dominant web background
-   Use heavy gradients throughout
-   Overuse lotus patterns
-   Add unnecessary shadows
-   Mix many font families
-   Make Gujarati typography decorative but unreadable
-   Create a generic marketplace layout
-   Overload pages with offers
-   Hide critical commerce information
-   Use unsupported heritage or quality claims

When someone lands on Patel Sweet Mart, the intended reaction is:

> premium and built for today."**

The site should appeal simultaneously to: - Existing loyal families -
Younger buyers - Gift shoppers - Gujarati diaspora - Corporate gifting
customers - First-time customers unfamiliar with the brand
